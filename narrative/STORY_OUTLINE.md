# Narrative Spine — Early Development

**Status:** Emotional premise confirmed; plot outline not locked.

## Past
Elias and his younger sister grow up in a dangerous but socially alive impoverished district. He acts as her guardian; she creates connections and seeks freedom.

## Foundational memory
She leads him from the market to a rooftop, where he briefly sees a beautiful world beyond their daily struggle.

## Inciting wound — working
They argue about his protectiveness. She leaves, and for once he does not pursue her. A violent incident strikes the neighborhood or her circle. She disappears; some children may be dead, missing or surviving. Exact circumstances are undecided.

## Present
Years or another interval later (open), Elias continues searching, burdened by guilt and without a stable sense of self. His journey encounters human conflicts, friendships, social systems and strange phenomena not reducible to his personal quest.

## Mira's independent life — open concept

Mira wants a future beyond the lower city: exploration, friendship, self-determination, and perhaps one day a family. A working idea is that she privately explores overlooked places and keeps small found objects. She does not frame this as a rebellion; if Elias notices something, she may simply say, "Oh, I just found it." This is exploratory, not canon. See [Mira — Everyday Life and Rooftop Scene](MIRA_EVERYDAY_LIFE.md).

The rooftop scene's emotional seed is that Mira sees possibility in the connected city while Elias sees distance and danger. Her question about whether their parents might be alive in the brighter heights leaves their fate open. The upper districts must not be assumed to be good, safe, evil, or welcoming simply because they are brighter. The rooftop's exact location and accessibility are also open; it need not be the highest place in the city.

## Unresolved structural questions
- Is the rooftop moment a playable prologue, flashback or later revelation?
- What forces shape the wider story beyond the search?
- What does the sister want, independently of Elias?
- How are player choices expressed, and which consequences persist?
- What ending would honor complexity without a simplistic cure?
