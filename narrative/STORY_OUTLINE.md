# Narrative Spine — Current Working Outline

> **Author-only: major ending spoilers.** This is a continuity reference, not finished prose. The authoritative status tracker is [CANON_STATUS.md](../decisions/CANON_STATUS.md). Older drafts are [archived](../archive/2026-10-17/STORY_OUTLINE_previous.md).

## Confirmed foundation

**Before the uprising:** Elias Varek, about 21, has effectively raised his sister Mira, 15, in a hidden shelter beneath the great viaducts. Their bond is loving and imperfect. His need to protect her can become controlling; she wants independence and her own friendships. Elias performs risky covert errands for a coercive lower-city faction, under leverage, to support their survival. A trusting childhood friend originally introduced him to the group.

**The birthday rooftop:** Mira brings Elias to a rooftop to show him the city. They argue about secrecy, protection and her autonomy. **They leave the rooftop together**, with the disagreement unresolved. Mira does not die there.

**The final assignment:** The faction promises Elias release after one last job. He believes he is reopening a restricted upper-city passage for clandestine movement. The leaders deliberately intend it to enable their planned uprising. Elias does **not** know he is facilitating an attack.

**The uprising:** Elias returns to chaos and civilians fleeing. He sees Mira among captives but cannot reach her. She is an ordinary civilian, not a chosen target or secret heir. She survives the initial capture and **dies later**; the cause, exact time and circumstances are undecided. Elias does not witness her death.

**Present, approximately two years later:** Elias is about 23 and searches for Mira, believing she may still be alive. He carries her silver pendant, his only remaining keepsake. Mira secretly read and marked his private journal while alive; Elias discovers her contribution only after her disappearance. That discovery is relational, not a clue that solves her fate.

**Ending:** Elias and the audience learn near the ending that Mira died. Allow time afterward for his emotional response. Her past perspective, if used, must remain truthful, and clues should be retrospectively fair.

## Character and thematic anchors

Elias is observant, capable at climbing and stealth, and intimately familiar with the city. He is compassionate but withdrawn, and struggles to receive affection. Mira is expressive, independent, affectionate and fully human beyond her relationship to him. The childhood friend is kind, trusting and susceptible to the faction's manipulation; name, survival and later arc are open. Themes include love versus control, guilt without false equivalence, grief, memory, responsibility, kindness and the possibility of living beyond one purpose.
