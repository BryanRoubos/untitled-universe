CHAPTER I / WHERE THE LIGHT FALLS

A City in Layers

A single city stretches toward the light while whole neighborhoods live beneath its shadow.

The city rises in layers. Bridges cross impossible distances between towers, roofs become roads, and entire neighborhoods gather beneath the weight of stone. To live here is to know that a short distance on a map can mean an hour of climbing.

High above, the wealthier districts receive open sky and sunlight. Far below, the streets are narrower and the daylight arrives in fragments. Yet the lower city is not merely darkness or ruin. It has markets, music, crowded homes, children, arguments, work, laughter and a thousand ordinary routines.

Its districts belong to one connected place, not separate worlds. Passages, stairways and viaducts connect people who may never understand one another.

The Arch · The city’s coin

Across the city’s bridges, workshops and crowded markets, people exchange everyday bronze coins called Arch coins. Their engraved monumental arch echoes the stone structures that connect the city’s levels. The coins pass from hand to hand, worn smooth by years of ordinary exchange.

Kareth · A month of the year

People in the city count their days using a calendar that includes a month called Kareth. The name appears in dates, letters and the private marks people leave behind.

CHAPTER II / WHERE THE LIGHT FALLS

Elias Varek

A young man who learned to keep someone else alive before he learned how to live for himself.

Elias knows the city by the routes that are not drawn. He remembers where a guard will look, which wall can be climbed and which roofs are safe after rain. These are not gifts granted by fate. They are skills learned through years of surviving.

At approximately twenty-three, he is attentive to other people’s suffering but struggles to accept their care in return. He can be gentle, perceptive and quietly funny. He can also disappear for days, break promises and push away those who try to get close.

He raised his younger sister, Mira, in the absence of their parents. Responsibility gave him purpose, but it also became a way of measuring his own worth. Protecting her could turn into controlling her. His love was real; so were the limits it placed on them both.

On Mira’s birthday, she persuades a reluctant Elias to go with her to a rooftop overlooking their city. He agrees because it is her birthday. They argue about his secrecy, his protectiveness and her need to make choices for herself. They leave together, but the disagreement remains unresolved.

Later, an uprising tears through the city. Mira is taken with other civilians, and Elias cannot reach her. Now he searches for his sister, holding on to the possibility that she survived.

Designing Elias

The clothing is practical and worn: a dirty-cream linen shirt, faded charcoal vest, gray-brown sash, dark trousers and weathered boots. The materials tell a story of repeated use, not heroic costume.

Personal Artifacts

Silver pendant. Mira owned it. Elias has it after her disappearance, and it is the only thing he has left of her. Where Mira got it, how it reached Elias and its exact design remain open.

Engraved silver ring. Present in an earlier visual reference, but not yet confirmed as a final personal possession. Its design, origin and significance remain open.

Weathered leather journal. Elias keeps this book deeply private. While she was alive, Mira secretly took it, read some of his thoughts and left a small drawing or message inside. He discovers what she did only after the uprising. The exact words or drawing, the pages she read, and the moment he finds it are still being developed.

CHAPTER IV / WHERE THE LIGHT FALLS

Lives Beneath the Arches

A city is made of the people who wake up in it each morning.

Before the streets became associated with catastrophe, they were simply home. Vendors argued over prices, neighbors traded favors, and children learned which alleys led to a friend’s door.

Life here is shaped by inequality, but its people are not defined solely by hardship. Nor is the upper city inhabited only by villains. The divisions are structural and political, lived through countless individual choices.

In the lower districts, a coercive organization controls parts of the local economy and exploits genuine grievances against unequal treatment from above. Its leaders pursue power for themselves, and most lower-city residents are not part of it. Their lives and loyalties cannot be reduced to the faction’s ambitions.

One of Elias’s oldest childhood friends, still unnamed, came to regard that organization as the family he never had. Trusting and eager to belong, he introduced Elias to its work without understanding how its leaders manipulated them. Their friendship had already suffered as Elias withdrew into his responsibilities for Mira. What happens to this friend later remains undecided.

A place to belong

The Home Beneath the Stone

Hidden within the foundations of the great viaducts, a small vaulted chamber became home to Elias and Mira. Beyond its walls, the city stretched upward for miles. Here, they found room for two lives.

CHAPTER V / WHERE THE LIGHT FALLS

The Search

Two years after the uprising, Elias still searches for the sister he lost in the chaos.

After the uprising, parts of the once-busy city lie damaged and scarred by violence. Yet beyond the ruined streets, other neighborhoods endure. People rebuild what they can, mourn those they have lost, and try to find a way forward in a city that no longer feels quite like home.

Elias moves through a city scarred by the uprising. During the violence, he glimpsed Mira among civilians being taken away and was unable to save her. He does not know what became of her afterward, and he believes she may still be somewhere beyond his reach.

The violence was not inevitable, and the decisions of those who planned and carried it out had consequences far beyond their own ambitions.

His journey is not about becoming a chosen hero. It is about memory, responsibility, the damage people do while trying to protect one another, and the possibility of a life beyond a single purpose.

The past follows him in fragments: a familiar street, a remembered argument, the sound of a voice he still expects to hear.

Some things remain with us long after the light has moved.

CHAPTER VI / WHERE THE LIGHT FALLS

The Artist’s Table

Sketches and studies from the making of this world.

This is a living manuscript. Some designs are approved; others are questions rendered in pencil and light. We keep both, but distinguish them clearly.

The world’s foundations—its story, characters, environments and creative ideas—come from Bryan Roubos. As a solo creator, Bryan begins with his own pencil drawings, sketches and ideas, then uses AI-assisted concept studies to explore and refine them into more detailed sketches and artwork. This process helps visualize possibilities and develop the project without spending months producing every exploratory image by hand. AI is a tool in that process, not the source of the project’s creative vision; individual studies remain concepts unless explicitly approved.

APPENDIX / CREATOR'S NOTES

The Workshop

The questions we are still asking, kept separate from the story we have already decided.

This appendix separates established foundations from artwork studies and unresolved questions. The numbered chapters are the reading experience; major revelations remain inside a closed disclosure.

Art & continuity

The chapters tell the story; this appendix tracks the decisions behind their illustrations. The city overview is a spatial concept, not a street map. The rooftop painting is a memory of the siblings together. The stone chamber was their home before the uprising; its furniture and exact passage layout remain visual exploration.

Established

People & places

Elias and Mira lived beneath the viaducts. The lower city remains inhabited, even though some districts suffered damage. Arch is the everyday bronze coin, and Kareth is a month name.

Visual studies

Artwork is not a map

The city panorama, markets, shelter storyboard, character studies and journal paintings guide appearance. They do not automatically establish exact locations, dates, dialogue, furniture, or incidental labels.

Creator-only story revelations

Ending spoiler: Mira died at fifteen after being captured in the uprising. Elias believes she may be alive and learns the truth with the audience near the ending.

Uprising spoiler: A coercive faction knowingly used Elias to open a passage for its attack. He believed he was performing clandestine work and did not knowingly begin the uprising.

Exact circumstances of Mira's death, the later journey, the faction's operational details and the final scenes are not yet fixed.

In development · Narrative

The road ahead

Elias’s encounters, turning points and the emotional shape of his journey still need to be written. Author-only ending details are kept in the collapsed spoiler section above.

In development · Characters

The childhood friend

The trusting friend who introduced Elias to the faction has no final name, fate or complete character arc yet.

In development · World

The city and its powers

The city’s name, government, faction name, detailed political structures and precise geography remain open. The upper and lower districts form one connected city.

In development · Mystery

Memory and the uncanny

Revisiting truthful events with additional context remains a possible narrative technique. Supernatural elements and their rules remain undecided.

In development · Design

Mira's final look

Her portrait studies are exploratory. Her final facial design, clothing, and many details of her independent daily life remain open.

In development · Format

Finding the right medium

The final form remains open. For now, this website presents the world as an illustrated concept book without committing the story to a game or novel.

This workshop is public. Story revelations are collapsed by default here, but the linked public GitHub repository contains full author-only notes and is not spoiler-safe.
