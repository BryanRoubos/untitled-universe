Environment development / Connected city studyCITY STRUCTURE / CONFIRMED

One city.
Many levels.

The sunlit monumental upper districts and the crowded lower neighborhoods belong to the same enormous, physically connected city. Vast stone arches, bridges, foundations and inhabited intermediate levels link them into one continuous urban environment.

The upper districts suggest grand architecture and prosperity; the lower districts retain their busy markets, improvised homes and community life. Neither district is defined by a simple good-versus-evil divide.

Confirmed: the upper and lower districts exist in one connected, vertically layered city. Exploratory: the illustration's labels, government and noble houses, cathedral functions, waterfalls, named divisions, precise layout and the origin of the viaducts. This is a spatial and atmospheric reference, not a finalized map. Click the artwork to enlarge.

Environment development / Location study 001LOWER CITY / EXPLORATION

Beneath the
great arches.

The approved visual direction for the impoverished district where Elias and his sister grew up: a living neighborhood sheltered beneath monumental, ancient stonework. Immense bridges and vaulted foundations rise above improvised dwellings, narrow walkways and crowded markets. Life persists here through hardship: families, vulnerable children, traders, friends and neighbors make a community among the shadows.

Visual direction selected for exploration; the city's name, exact geography, architecture history and political structure are not canon. Click either artwork to enlarge.

Environment development / Location study 002CHILDREN'S SHELTER / VISUAL STUDY

A place only
they knew.

Hidden within the lower city's enormous stone foundations, this proposed childhood refuge is cold, damp and painfully makeshift. The children have done what they can with scavenged bedding, crates, cloth and a little candlelight. Their attempts at comfort make the hardship more visible, not less.

Our selected design explores a neglected storage alcove where loose stones conceal a child-sized crawlspace. A low passage bends out of sight before reaching a small, enclosed chamber. A narrow high vent lets in cold daylight, without exposing the shelter to the busy walkways outside. The concealed approach is an important part of the concept: it should look unremarkable to everyone except the children who know the way.

Approved visual exploration, not finalized architecture or story canon. The shelter's precise location, history, occupants, and role in the disappearance remain unwritten. Click the sketch to zoom into the panels.
